## 3. Market research

### 3.1 Demand signals (2026)

- **SMB adoption is still early, which leaves room for implementers.** Census BTOS puts AI use at 17–20% of US businesses (Dec 2025–May 2026), with 20–23% expecting to use it within six months. Adoption reaches 37% at 250+ employees but stays under 20% for firms with four or fewer.
- **SMB agent piloting is broad.** Upwork Research Institute (Q1 2026, SMB leaders with 10–99 employees) found that leaders piloting AI agents outnumber those "not considering" in every function surveyed. Only about 3% are not considering workflow automation.
- **Agentic adoption among SMBs is doubling.** First Page Sage reports 7% → 14% for 50–499-employee firms (2025 → 2026), with abandonment at 43%. The rising adoption plus high failure rate is DIO's opening.
- **Budgets are rising.** 88% of executives plan to raise AI budgets because of agentic AI (PwC AI Agent Survey). Fewer than half are redesigning processes around agents (42–45%), and process redesign is an operations skill.
- **The agent market is growing about 43–50% a year** (MarketsandMarkets: $7.84B in 2025 → $52.6B in 2030). Much of that is vendor-embedded agents, which *increases* the need for someone to connect, govern and audit them.
- **Regulation and privacy pressure.** Florida's 2026 session debated CS/SB 482, the "Artificial Intelligence Bill of Rights" (bot disclosure, deidentified-data duties); it passed the Senate but **died in the House on March 13, 2026** (see §3.2b below). The 2023 Florida Digital Bill of Rights still applies to the largest companies. Healthcare and legal buyers face HIPAA, privilege and confidentiality limits. Together these create demand for **private, governed, self-hosted AI**, which matches DIO's stack.

### 3.2 Local market: Palm Beach County / South Florida

- Palm Beach County added **891 net establishments in 2026 Q1** (FAU South Florida Economic Report), so the base of new and growing small businesses is healthy.
- South Florida is dense in **professional services** (law, CPA and wealth management, medical and dental practices, real estate and property management, insurance) and **field services** (landscaping, tree care, construction, HVAC, marine). These are the target verticals ([§5](03-target-customers.md)).
- **Network access points:** FAU College of Business (MBA cohort, alumni), the Florida SBDC network (hosted at FAU in this region) and local chambers of commerce. FPL/NextEra contacts and its vendor ecosystem are **off limits** while Erich is employed there ([§13](11-risks.md)).
- **Named local prospects:** [Prospect research: businesses within 10 miles of Boynton Beach](local-prospects.md) (November 2026) ranks 542 local businesses. The strongest are independent law firms, community association managers, dental and medical groups, insurance agencies and title companies. It also covers Florida's 2026 condominium website requirement, which makes community association managers the most timely segment.

### 3.2b Florida rules that move demand (checked November 25, 2026)

- **Condominium and HOA websites.** Since January 1, 2026, condominium associations with 25 or more units must post official records on a website or app (HB 1021, Ch. 2024-244; HOAs with 100 or more parcels have a similar duty). The community association managers who serve them carry the records and owner-request load: the most timely local segment.
- **No general Florida AI law yet.** The "Artificial Intelligence Bill of Rights" (CS/SB 482) passed the Senate on March 4, 2026 but **died in the House on March 13, 2026** ([flsenate.gov](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026/482)). The AI law that did pass, **SB 484** (signed May 7, 2026, effective July 1, 2026), regulates hyperscale data centers, not how small businesses use AI ([WUSF](https://www.wusf.org/politics-issues/2026-05-07/desantis-signs-bill-adding-regulations-for-hyper-scale-ai-data-centers)). Buyers still face HIPAA, attorney confidentiality, the Florida Information Protection Act and the FTC's rules on AI claims, so the case for **private, governed AI** is unchanged. Earlier drafts of the business plan cited SB 482 as advancing; don't cite it to clients as law.
