# Local prospects: segments within 10 miles of Boynton Beach

*Public summary of the prospect research brief in the private DIO operating manual (gen-skeptic/experts, `research/boynton-beach-prospect-research.md`, compiled November 21, 2026). This repository is public, so the named list of businesses, the CSV and every business name have been left out on purpose; the full brief stays in the private repository. What remains is the method, the local market size and the ranking of segments, which is what the expert agents need to advise.*

**What the full brief covers:** every business we could find within 10 miles of Boynton Beach in the industries DIO serves (542 in total, 17 in tier A and 150 in tier B), ranked from most to least likely to buy. This summary keeps the method and the segment ranking.

**How to read it:** the ranking is desk research from public listings, Census data and company websites, not conversations. Treat it as an order for outreach, not a forecast of who will buy.

---

## 1. Summary

- **The best prospects are independent, owner-led firms with 10 to 100 staff** in personal-injury and elder law, community association management, multi-dentist and multi-provider practices, independent insurance agencies and title companies.
- **Community association management is the most timely segment.** Since January 1, 2026, Florida requires every condominium association with 25 or more units to post its official documents on a website or app (the old threshold was 150 units); HOAs with 100 or more parcels have a similar duty. The managers who serve these associations carry the records, posting and owner-communication load ([HB 1021, Ch. 2024-244](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2024/1021)).
- **Law firms are the largest pool:** 73 firms within the radius, 43 of them in tiers A and B. Census data suggests about 90 legal establishments with 10 to 99 employees inside the radius *(est.)*.
- **Least likely to buy:** national chains and PE-backed groups, franchises and captive insurance agencies, hospitals, government and large headquarters, and solo practitioners.
- **Employer boundary:** no utility, vegetation-management or line-clearance contractor, and no company known to be an FPL/NextEra vendor, is on the list. All 75 field-service businesses (HVAC, plumbing, roofing and pest control) are flagged for Erich's screen before scoping; the three that do electrical work carry a stricter flag: decline if unsure.

## 2. Scope and method

**Area.** The area is a 10-mile straight-line radius from Boynton Beach City Hall. It covers:

- Boynton Beach, Delray Beach, Lantana and Lake Worth Beach
- Greenacres, Atlantis, Hypoluxo, Ocean Ridge, Gulf Stream and Highland Beach
- the unincorporated western communities of Boynton Beach, Delray Beach and Lake Worth
- north Boca Raton, along the Yamato Road business corridor

Downtown West Palm Beach, central Boca Raton and most of Wellington are outside the radius.

**Sources, all retrieved November 20 and 21, 2026:**

- **US Census Bureau:**
  - [County Business Patterns 2023](https://api.census.gov/data/2023/cbp) for Palm Beach County, giving establishments by industry and size class.
  - [ACS 2019–2023 five-year estimates](https://api.census.gov/data/2023/acs/acs5) for population by county subdivision.
- **Maps business listings, through Firecrawl.** We ran 36 searches by industry and city across Boynton Beach, Delray Beach, Lake Worth Beach, Lantana, Greenacres and north Boca Raton. They returned 648 listings, of which 590 are within 10 miles.
- **Company websites:** we read the homepages of 28 of the highest-ranked businesses for team size, offices, year founded and the tools they already use.
- **[City of Boynton Beach Annual Comprehensive Financial Report, FY2024](https://flauditor.gov/pages/mun_efile%20rpts/2024%20boynton%20beach.pdf):** the principal employers table.
- **One commercial firmographics provider** (three firms looked up). Its data was partial and one match was wrong, so we used it only where the company's own website agreed.

**Cleaning.**

- We merged listings for the same business, using the same website or the same office.
- We removed one closed listing.
- We set aside managed IT providers and agencies, because they are channel partners (business plan [§7.3](09-marketing.md)), not prospects.
- We added six large local employers from public sources.

The result is 542 businesses.

**Scoring (100 points).** Every input is a public proxy; the score ranks outreach order, it doesn't predict revenue.

| Factor | Points | How we measured it |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Segment fit | 30 | Match to the ideal customer profile in the [business plan (§5)](03-target-customers.md): law and community association management score highest, then property management and CPA firms, title, insurance, medical, dental and the rest. |
| Workflow pain | 10 | How much intake, scheduling, document or records work the segment carries; +1 for several offices; +1 for a community association manager with public ratings of 3.0 or lower, which signals strain in owner communication. |
| Scale | 30 | Total public review count across the business's local listings, used as a stand-in for client volume. For segments whose clients rarely leave reviews (CPA, title, wealth, staffing ×2; insurance and behavioral health ×1.5) the count is multiplied before banding. +3 for several offices. Where a website check showed a team of one to three professionals, scale is capped at 13; where it showed 40 or more staff, scale is at least 27. |
| Ownership and decision access | 20 | Independent and local 20; independent but headquartered elsewhere or statewide 10; possibly backed by a dental service organization 6; franchise or captive agency 6; nonprofit 8; corporate, PE-backed, national chain, hospital or government 0. Judged from the name, website and listing; marked where we verified it. |
| Proximity | 10 | 3 miles or less 10; up to 6 miles 8; up to 8 miles 6; up to 10 miles 4. In-person work is easier close to home. |
| Employer-boundary deduction | −5 / −10 | −5 for field services, which need Erich's screen before scoping; −10 where the business does electrical work. |

**Tiers:** A is 85 or more, B is 72 to 84, C is 58 to 71 and D is below 58.

## 3. Local market size

Of the 55,803 establishments with employees in Palm Beach County, these are the ones in DIO's core industries (Census CBP 2023). The 10–99 employee band is the target range in the ideal customer profile.

| Industry (NAICS) | County establishments | With 10–99 employees | Within the radius, 10–99 employees *(est.)* |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Legal services (5411) | 2,090 | 220 | about 90 |
| Offices of physicians (6211) | 2,170 | 484 | about 195 |
| Offices of dentists (6212) | 810 | 185 | about 75 |
| Other health practitioners: therapy, chiropractic, etc. (6213) | 1,751 | 130 | about 50 |
| Plumbing, heating and air-conditioning contractors (238220) | 711 | 133 | about 55 |
| Insurance agencies and brokerages (5242) | 1,265 | 117 | about 45 |
| Accounting, tax and bookkeeping (5412) | 1,285 | 94 | about 40 |
| Residential property managers (531311) | 466 | 64 | about 25 |
| **Total** | **10,548** | **1,427** | **about 570** |

**How we estimated the radius share:** the Boynton Beach–Delray Beach and Lake Worth county subdivisions hold 579,388 of the county's 1,507,453 residents (38%, ACS 2019–2023). The radius also takes in parts of the Boca Raton, Sunshine Parkway and West Palm Beach subdivisions, so we apply 40%. Offices cluster in downtown West Palm Beach and Boca Raton, both outside the radius, so read these figures as approximate. Community association managers have no NAICS code of their own; most file under property management or other services, so the Census can't count them separately.

**What this means:** about 570 firms in the target size band sit within 10 miles *(est.)*. Our list names 542 businesses of all sizes and contains 167 in tiers A and B. The pool is large enough for a side practice that needs three to five clients a year, so being selective costs us little.

## 6. Least likely to buy, and why

| Group | Why | Better route |
| --- | --- | --- |
| National chains and PE-backed groups | IT and vendors are chosen at headquarters | None locally |
| Franchises and captive agencies | The franchisor or carrier controls systems and data | Franchisee-owned add-ons only, case by case |
| Hospitals, government and large corporate headquarters | Procurement, security review and central IT; far above the 10–100 staff target | Workshops (F), or as a subcontractor to an established vendor |
| Senior living communities | Most are run by national or regional operators | None locally |
| Solo practitioners, individual agents and small home-care agencies | Too little volume or budget for a build | Workshops (F) and content; refer to a productized offer later |
| Self-managed condo and HOA associations | Volunteer boards and small budgets | Reach them through their management company (segment 1) |

## 7. Exclusions, flags and partners

- **Excluded:** utilities, vegetation-management and line-clearance contractors, and any company known to be an FPL/NextEra vendor. None of the searches targeted those categories.
- **Flagged for Erich's screen (75):** every HVAC, plumbing, roofing and pest-control business. The three that do electrical work carry the stricter "decline if unsure" note. A flagged deal can't reach scoping until Erich clears it.
- **Partners, not prospects:** seven managed IT providers and one agency nearby fit the partner channel ([marketing](09-marketing.md), §7.3). They are not in the ranked list.

## 8. Recommended next steps

1. **Load tier A and the top of tier B into the CRM** (in the private operating manual), flag every row that needs Erich's screen, and add contacts only with their consent status.
2. **Lead with warm channels:** the local chambers of commerce, the Florida SBDC at FAU, local chapters of the bar, CPA, dental and community-association professional associations, and referrals from partner MSPs. A free "fix first" workshop for community association managers and law-firm administrators (Line F) is the natural opener.
3. **First three campaigns (our recommendation):**
   1. **Community association managers:** handling 2026 records and owner requests (A, H, then B).
   2. **Personal-injury, elder and family law firms:** governed intake and drafting on a private stack (A, B, C).
   3. **Multi-dentist and multi-provider practices:** recall and front-office workflows on a stack that can sign a BAA (A, B, C).
4. **Verify team size and ownership** for every unverified business before its fit call.
5. **Refresh every quarter,** and widen the radius or add remote segments ([marketing](09-marketing.md), §8) once the local tier A list is worked through.
